## Fact-check (Google Business Profile Help, read 2026-11-16)
**Guidelines for representing your business on Google** (support.google.com/business/answer/3038177):
- "Your name should reflect your business's real-world name, as used consistently on your storefront, website, stationery, and as known to customers."
- "Including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted, and could result in the suspension of your Business Profile."
- Names must not include marketing taglines, business hours information, or service/product or location information unless it's part of the real name.
  - The page's own acceptable examples include genuine names with service words, such as "Verizon Wireless" and "Advance Auto Parts".
- Categories: "Do not use categories solely as keywords."
- Hours, service areas and categories each have their own fields and rules.

**Fix suspended or disabled profiles** (support.google.com/business/answer/4569145):
- "We may suspend or disable Business Profiles that don't follow our guidelines."

**What the episode avoids saying:** that suspension is guaranteed or immediate, that descriptive real names are banned, that filling fields guarantees ranking, or that the fictional warnings are real Google messages.

## Limitations
1. **Runtime is 74.3 s against a 55–70 s target.**
   - The supplied recording is 66.5 s, and about 64 s of it is dialogue.
   - Reaching 70 s would have meant cutting the comedic pauses the brief asks for, or rushing speech.
   - The longest single pause is a natural 1.3 s gap inside the recorded L07, between "...real business name." and "Unnecessary keywords...".
2. **One voice for three roles.** "Jack John" voices the owner, the Maps Assistant and the narrator; they are distinct by pitch.
3. **"47 words" in the ribbon.** The ribbon is designed to hold exactly 47 extra words: 9 spoken plus 38 in the ribbon. Because the ribbon text flows and loops, not all 38 are on screen at once. The counter carries the +47.
4. **Synthesized music and SFX.** They are synthesized in-project, with no licensed audio and no paid services.
